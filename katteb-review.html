Katteb review: the fast answer

⚡ Information gain — 30-second summary

Katteb is the rare AI writer that treats citations as the product — every article ships with in-text sources you can verify — and the AppSumo lifetime deal starts at $39 for 15,000 credits a month with 900+ reviews, a sensible pick for bloggers and agencies who publish fact-heavy content.

That is the short version. The longer version is about what “fact-checked” really means in practice — and where Katteb earns and loses trust.

Our analysis shows Katteb is strongest at long-form SEO articles, rewriting, and now scheduled AutoBlogging straight into WordPress. It is not a magic bullet for originality, and heavy users should do the math on credits before stacking ten codes.

What Katteb actually does

Katteb was founded in September 2021 in Dubai by Ahmed (the founder, who ran a digital marketing agency for eight years before starting it) and it is funded with $100,000, with customer data stored on servers in France. The platform has rebuilt itself around what it calls “the AI Search era,” where content needs to show up in Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT answers — not just rank in classic search.

The feature set splits into four areas:

  • Fact-checked article writer — long-form articles up to 2,500 words with in-text citations and related images drawn from sources you choose
  • Rewriter & InLinks — rewrite any web page or text in 110+ languages while preserving formatting, and add internal links without leaving the app
  • AutoBlogging — plan a month of topics, schedule posts, and auto-publish directly to WordPress
  • AI SEO Assistant & research tools — check missing topics, entities and competitor gaps while you write, plus trend discovery

There is also Katteb Chat, an AI designer for images, brand voice settings, writing styles, a humanizer, a fact checker, and a public API for programmatic content — the API ships at no extra charge and was announced in a founder update on the AppSumo page.

How we tested the platform

Testing an AI writer means reading the output, not just generating it. We ran a controlled protocol:

Article: generate a 1,500-word fact-heavy article and spot-check every citation against its source

Rewrite: rewrite a published page and compare structure and factual drift

AutoBlog + API: schedule a test post to a staging WordPress site and call the API for a short generation

The result: citations checked out on the articles we generated, the API behaved exactly like the dashboard, and the WordPress auto-publish flow is the real differentiator for anyone running a content calendar.

Feature deep dive

The fact-checked article writer

You start with a title, pick a language (110+ supported), audience geography, length from 500 to 2,500 words, and up to three data sources — the internet, a specific website link, Amazon, or YouTube. Katteb then drafts the article with in-text citations and related images.

In our testing, the citations were the differentiator. Almost every factual claim carried a numbered source, and the quality of the sourcing followed the quality of the sources you allow it to use. The YouTube Writer is a nice extra: paste a video URL and generate content derived from the transcript.

AutoBlogging and the WordPress pipeline

AutoBlogging is where Katteb stops being a “writer” and becomes a publishing system: plan topics ahead, schedule a full month of posts, and auto-publish to WordPress. The founder frames it as the fix for “less manual work and much more consistency,” and in our staging test the pipeline worked as advertised.

The AI SEO assistant and brand voice

The rebuilt platform adds an SEO assistant that checks missing topics, entities and competitor gaps while you write, then suggests fast fixes for weak sections. Brand settings help the output match your voice, and a fact checker/humanizer pair rounds out the trust toolkit. For the AI-search era, Katteb can also add citations, patents and credibility signals to make articles more quotable by LLMs.

Add a title (or use Katteb’s suggestions), choose the language and audience geography, pick a length from 500 to 2,500 words, select up to three data sources (internet, website links, Amazon or YouTube), and generate. The article comes back with citations and related images.
